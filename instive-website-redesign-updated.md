# Instive AI — Website Redesign Blueprint
## From "AI agency portfolio" → "AI-powered supply chain workflows"

---

## Executive Summary

Your current site is strong — well-written copy, real mockups, good problem-first structure. But it reads as a **consulting firm with AI capabilities**, not as a **AI automation company that delivers outcomes**. Reform's site succeeds because every pixel communicates one thing: "we are the automation operating system for freight, and our customers have the numbers to prove it." This redesign closes that gap.

---

## 1. Diagnosis: What's Working, What's Not

### What your site does well (keep these)
- **Problem-first solution framing** ("Eliminate manual invoice audits") — this is genuinely better than Reform's more generic "Workflow Automation. Built for Logistics."
- **Live mockups** (Air-Sea Forwarders, READY-2-XECUTE, Paris Brothers) — unique, no competitor does this. These are your best sales asset.
- **Quantified outcomes** ($96.2K recoverable, 88% dispute win rate, 91.6% forecast accuracy)
- **Four-step journey** (discovery → prototype → pilot → scale) — honest, builds trust
- **Industry ticker** ("Freight forwarding · 3PL · Cold chain…") — immediately signals domain expertise

### What needs to change

| Problem | Current state | What Reform does differently |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity crisis** | You describe yourself as "builds intelligent systems" — this could be any AI consultancy | Reform says "Workflow Automation. Built for Logistics." — product-first, vertical-specific. One sentence, done. |
| **No product shown** | Not a single screenshot of your actual platform. Your mockups show *client outcomes*, but the buyer also wants to see the system they'll use daily | Reform shows dashboard screenshots, workflow UI, document processing screens. The product is visible and real. |
| **"We build AI" framing** | Your copy positions you as builders-for-hire: "Custom AI agents, designed around your data" | Reform positions themselves as a product: "Automate your supply chain operations with specialized AI" — the client adopts a platform, not hires a team |
| **Tech stack section is inward-facing** | "Layer 1: Data sources → Layer 2: Ingestion → Layer 3: Data platform" — this reads like an architecture doc, not a value prop | Reform shows integrations (CargoWise, QuickBooks, Outlook, Gmail logos) — the buyer sees *their* tools, not your internals |
| **Hero is generic** | "Intelligence that optimizes every move" — could be any AI company | Reform's hero is a product screenshot carousel showing the actual workflow in action |
| **Too many sections** | 8 numbered sections + footer + floating CTA — scroll fatigue | Reform has fewer, punchier sections with more whitespace |
| **No case studies** | Your /case-studies page exists but has no real published case studies with named companies | Reform has named, detailed case studies (KCE, Interlink, Joffroy) with quantified results |
| **"Where we are going" section undermines trust** | Telling prospects "today we build custom; someday we'll be a platform" implies the product doesn't exist yet | Reform never says "someday" — they present what exists today as complete |

## 2. The Core Repositioning

### Current positioning
> "We're an AI company that builds intelligent systems for your supply chain."
> (Translation: we're consultants with AI skills)

### New positioning
> "We're the AI workflow partner for supply chain operations. We design and deploy AI-powered workflows tailored to your operations."
> (Translation: we're a AI automation partner that delivers outcomes)

### The one-liner test
Reform: "Workflow Automation. Built for Logistics."
Turian: "Your AI Agent for B2B workflow automation."
Kognitos: "The Deterministic Agentic AI Workflow for the Enterprise."

**Instive should be:**
> **"AI-powered process automation. Built for supply chain."**

or

> **"The AI workflow partner for supply chain operations."**

Short. Product-first. Vertical-specific.

## 5. Design Direction

### 5.1 What to change visually

| Element | Current | New direction |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Dark (near-black #0A0A0A) | Light (#FAFAF8 or clean white) — Reform, Turian, and Kognitos all use light backgrounds. Dark themes signal "developer tool"; light themes signal "enterprise SaaS." Your buyers are ops managers, not engineers. |
| Color palette | Amber/gold accents on dark | Deep blue primary (#0E4DA4 or similar navy), supporting teal for success states, warm amber for highlights. Blue = trust, stability, enterprise. Every supply chain SaaS that wins enterprise deals uses blue or navy. |
| Typography | Clean sans-serif (good, keep the family) | Increase heading weight/size contrast. The hero headline should be larger and more declarative. |
| Imagery | Container port photos, warehouse stock imagery, concept art | Product screenshots as hero imagery. Stock photos only in blog/about, never on the homepage. |
| Product shots | Zero | Must have 4-5 polished workflow screenshots (portal dashboard, doc review, approval queue, analytics, workflow run). If real product isn't ready, build high-fidelity mockups in Figma — these are your most important marketing assets. |
| Whitespace | Dense — 8 numbered sections feel packed | Fewer sections, more breathing room between them. Reform's homepage has ~6 content sections with generous spacing. |
| Animations | Hero video, scrolling ticker | Minimal. Product screenshot carousel with clean transitions. Subtle scroll reveals. No ambient videos. |

### 5.2 Must-have visual assets (create these first)

1. **Workflow dashboard screenshot** (operations view with KPIs, exception queue, run status)
2. **Document review screenshot** (side-by-side: original PDF + extracted fields with confidence scores)
3. **Approval queue screenshot** (task list with one-tap approve/edit/reject, SLA timers)
4. **Analytics screenshot** (charts: touchless rate trend, cycle time, volume by type)
5. **Integration diagram** (simple: your AI workflows in the center, logo connections radiating out)
6. **Customer logos** (even 3 is better than zero — get these from your design partners immediately)

- **Your current tone:** literary, atmospheric, almost novelistic. "We read what your operation already knows, sense what is about to go wrong, and act before it costs you."
- **New tone:** keep the intelligence and warmth, drop the poetry. Be as direct as Reform but with the domain vocabulary that shows you *know* supply chain. You should sound like the smartest ops VP in the room, not like a creative writing grad.

**Current:** "Instive AI builds intelligent systems for supply chain, procurement, logistics, warehousing and distribution."

## 9. What Makes Reform's Site Feel "Clean and Telling"

The specific design patterns to adopt:

1. **Product-first hero:** The first thing you see is the workflow in action, not an abstract animation. The headline states what the product does, not a vision statement.

2. **Logo bar immediately after hero:** "These real companies use us" — before the buyer has to scroll. Trust front-loaded.

3. **Aggregate metrics in a single row:** "20M+ tasks automated · $500M+ invoices · 250,000+ hours saved" — proof of scale in 3 seconds.

4. **Few sections, generous whitespace:** Reform's homepage has maybe 6-7 content blocks with lots of vertical breathing room. Feels premium, not busy.

5. **Testimonials as first-class content:** Named people, real photos, company logos, and a key metric per testimonial. Not tucked away — given a full section with a carousel.

6. **Integration logos, not architecture diagrams:** The buyer sees CargoWise, QuickBooks, Gmail — tools they already use. Not "Layer 3: Data platform."

7. **Templates section:** "Start with a template and go live in weeks" — signals a product library, not custom builds. Your blueprint catalog maps perfectly to this.

8. **Single, repeated CTA:** "Book a Demo" appears 3 times — hero, middle, bottom. Same button, same destination. No confusion.

9. **Security badge in footer:** SOC 2 logo, quietly building institutional trust.

10. **Light background, clean typography, blue-toned palette:** Professional, enterprise, trustworthy.

Adopt all ten of these. Your content is already competitive — the gap is presentation and positioning.
